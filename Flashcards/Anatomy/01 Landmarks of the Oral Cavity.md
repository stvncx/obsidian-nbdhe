# Landmarks of the Oral Cavity

## Vestibule, gingiva & surrounding structures

Parotid papilla:::Small elevation opposite the maxillary 2nd molars; opening of Stensen's duct (parotid gland)

The parotid papilla is the opening of ==Stensen's== duct and sits opposite the maxillary ==second molars==.

Maxillary tuberosity:::Elevation behind the most posterior maxillary molar

Retromolar pad:::Dense pad behind the most posterior mandibular molar

Which structure is behind the last molar — maxillary tuberosity or retromolar pad?
?
Both, on different arches:
- **Maxillary tuberosity**: behind the last *maxillary* molar
- **Retromolar pad**: behind the last *mandibular* molar

Vestibule::Horseshoe-shaped space between the cheeks, lips, and teeth

The ==mucobuccal fold== is the line where the buccal mucosa meets the alveolar mucosa.

Labial frenum::Vertical band that attaches the cheeks and lips to the alveolar mucosa

Alveolar mucosa vs. attached gingiva — how do they look and behave?
?
- **Alveolar mucosa**: redder, thinner, easily stretched; overlies bone
- **Attached gingiva**: thick band bound to the tooth and alveolar bone; may show pigmentation

Marginal gingiva is also called ==free gingiva== — the non-attached gingiva toward the crown.

The mucogingival junction (MGJ) is where the ==attached gingiva== meets the ==alveolar mucosa==.

Interdental gingiva is also called the::Interdental papilla (gingiva between the teeth)

## Palate

Hard palate vs. soft palate — appearance?
?
- **Hard palate**: firmer, whiter, anterior
- **Soft palate**: yellower, smaller, softer, posterior

Incisive papilla:::Small bulge behind the maxillary central incisors

Palatine rugae:::Firm, irregular wave-like ridges on the surface of the palate

The palatine raphe runs from the ==uvula== to the ==incisive papilla== and is evidence of the fusion of the ==palatal processes== during fetal development.

Uvula::Projection from the posterior edge of the soft palate

Pterygomandibular fold — what is it and where does it run?
?
Fold of tissue from the junction of the hard and soft palate on each side, down to the mandible just posterior to the most distal mandibular molar.

## Tongue & floor of the mouth

The base of the tongue is the posterior ==one-third==; the body is the anterior ==two-thirds==.

Which tongue papillae contain NO taste buds?::Filiform papillae (they have tactile receptors only)

Filiform papillae are missing in which condition?::Median rhomboid glossitis

Name the 4 types of lingual papillae and one feature of each.
?
- **Filiform**: most numerous, dorsal surface; tactile, *no* taste buds
- **Fungiform**: mushroom-shaped, appear as red dots
- **Circumvallate**: largest; 10–14 in a row along the sulcus terminalis; >100 taste buds each
- **Foliate**: on the lateral sides of the tongue

Circumvallate papillae number ==10–14==, line the ==sulcus terminalis==, and each holds over ==100== taste buds.

Sulcus terminalis::V-shaped groove separating the body and base of the tongue

Foramen cecum:::Depression in the midline of the tongue, at the point of the V-shaped sulcus terminalis

Lingual tonsil — location?::Far back of the tongue, posterior to the circumvallate papillae; paired, irregular lymphoid tissue

Sublingual caruncle:::Duct opening for the submandibular and sublingual salivary glands

Lingual veins (lingual varicosities) are dark veins on the ==ventral== surface of the tongue, more visible in ==elderly== patients.

Plica fimbriata::Bilateral fold next to the lingual veins on the ventral tongue

Lingual frenum::Band of tissue between the ventral tongue and the floor of the mouth

## Neck & related structures

Name the 3 divisions of the pharynx, superior to inferior.
?
1. **Nasopharynx**: superior to the soft palate; contains the nasal cavity
2. **Oropharynx**: between the soft palate and the opening of the pharynx
3. **Laryngopharynx**: below the oropharynx; *not* visible intraorally

Which division of the pharynx is NOT visible during an intraoral exam?::Laryngopharynx

Epiglottis — what does it do?
?
Cartilage flap below the base of the tongue. Stays vertical at rest; covers the entrance of the larynx during swallowing to prevent aspiration.

The anterior faucial pillar is formed by the ==palatoglossus== muscle; the posterior faucial pillar by the ==palatopharyngeal== muscle.

Palatine tonsils sit between the::Anterior and posterior faucial pillars (palatoglossal and palatopharyngeal folds)

Hyoid bone::Bone suspended in the neck, superior to the thyroid cartilage

Thyroid cartilage is also called the ==Adam's apple== and is ==superior== to the thyroid gland.

Thyroid gland::Butterfly-shaped gland in the neck; the parathyroid glands (4) lie on it
