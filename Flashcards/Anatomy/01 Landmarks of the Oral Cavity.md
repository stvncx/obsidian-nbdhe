# Landmarks of the Oral Cavity

## Exam-style

Which structure marks the opening of Stensen's (parotid) duct?
A. Sublingual caruncle
B. Parotid papilla
C. Incisive papilla
D. Plica fimbriata
?
**B. Parotid papilla**, on the buccal mucosa opposite the maxillary 2nd molars.
The sublingual caruncle is where the submandibular and sublingual ducts open.

A patient has a smooth, red, rhomboid-shaped area on the midline of the dorsal tongue, just anterior to the circumvallate papillae. Which papillae are absent from this area?
A. Circumvallate
B. Foliate
C. Filiform
D. Fungiform
?
**C. Filiform**: median rhomboid glossitis. Filiform papillae give the dorsal tongue its rough, whitish texture; without them the area looks smooth and red.

Which lingual papillae line the sulcus terminalis and contain the most taste buds?
A. Circumvallate
B. Filiform
C. Fungiform
D. Foliate
?
**A. Circumvallate**: 10–14 large papillae in a V along the sulcus terminalis, each with more than 100 taste buds.

The anterior faucial pillar is formed by which muscle?
A. Superior pharyngeal constrictor
B. Palatopharyngeus
C. Styloglossus
D. Palatoglossus
?
**D. Palatoglossus** (anterior pillar). The posterior pillar is the palatopharyngeus.
Memory hook: pala-to-**gloss**us runs to the tongue, which is in front.

The palatine tonsils are located between the
A. lingual tonsil and the epiglottis.
B. anterior and posterior faucial pillars.
C. uvula and the soft palate.
D. pterygomandibular fold and the retromolar pad.
?
**B. Anterior and posterior faucial pillars** (palatoglossal and palatopharyngeal folds).

Which part of the pharynx can be seen during an intraoral examination?
A. Nasopharynx
B. Oropharynx
C. Laryngopharynx
D. None of the pharynx is visible
?
**B. Oropharynx**: between the soft palate and the opening of the pharynx. The nasopharynx is above the soft palate; the laryngopharynx is below the oropharynx.

Apical to the mucogingival junction, the tissue is redder, thinner, and moves when the lip is stretched. This tissue is
A. attached gingiva.
B. marginal gingiva.
C. alveolar mucosa.
D. interdental papilla.
?
**C. Alveolar mucosa**: loosely bound, so it is movable. Attached gingiva is firmly bound to the tooth and bone, so it does not move.

A 78-year-old patient has dark, bluish, tortuous vessels on the ventral surface of the tongue. They are asymptomatic. These are most likely
A. a hemangioma.
B. lingual varicosities.
C. a ranula.
D. a mucocele.
?
**B. Lingual varicosities**: dilated lingual veins, a normal age-related finding. No treatment needed; document them.

Each of the following is found on the ventral tongue or floor of the mouth EXCEPT one. Which is the EXCEPTION?
A. Lingual frenum
B. Plica fimbriata
C. Sublingual caruncle
D. Foramen cecum
?
**D. Foramen cecum**: on the *dorsal* tongue, in the midline at the point of the V-shaped sulcus terminalis.

Which landmark guides needle insertion for an inferior alveolar nerve block?
A. Pterygomandibular raphe
B. Mucobuccal fold
C. Maxillary tuberosity
D. Retromolar pad
?
**A. Pterygomandibular raphe (fold)**: the needle enters lateral to it, at the height of the coronoid notch.

## Must-know facts

Stensen's duct opens opposite which teeth?::Maxillary 2nd molars (at the parotid papilla)

Which salivary ducts open at the sublingual caruncle?::Submandibular (Wharton's) and sublingual ducts

How many circumvallate papillae are there?::10–14

The base of the tongue is the posterior ==one-third==; the body is the anterior ==two-thirds==.

The mucogingival junction is where the ==attached gingiva== meets the ==alveolar mucosa==.

How do you calculate the width of the attached gingiva?
?
Distance from the gingival margin to the mucogingival junction, **minus** the probing depth.

Pterygomandibular fold — what is it and where does it run?
?
Fold of tissue from the junction of the hard and soft palate on each side, down to the mandible just posterior to the most distal mandibular molar.

## Tell apart

Maxillary tuberosity vs. retromolar pad?
?
Both are behind the last molar, on different arches:
- **Maxillary tuberosity**: bony elevation behind the last *maxillary* molar
- **Retromolar pad**: dense soft-tissue pad behind the last *mandibular* molar

Incisive papilla vs. palatine rugae vs. palatine raphe?
?
- **Incisive papilla**: small bulge right behind the maxillary central incisors
- **Palatine rugae**: firm, irregular, wave-like ridges across the anterior palate
- **Palatine raphe**: midline line from the incisive papilla back to the uvula (where the palatal processes fused)
